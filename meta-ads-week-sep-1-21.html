A figure at or above 100% is impossible by definition: Meta can't have driven more revenue than the category earned in total. Compost and Firewood both fail that test this period, worse than last time (Compost was 421% in the Sep 1–13 report, now 335% on the fuller month; Firewood has gone from just over the line at 101% to a decisive 173%). Heating oil (4%) and Sand+Stones (12%) sit in a range that could plausibly be real, though neither is confirmed: none of the real orders in either category this period are actually tagged to Meta.
Where the inflation comes fromMost of it is view-through: purchases Meta credits itself for because the person saw an ad without clicking it. Re-running this period's figures on a click-only basis (only counting people who actually clicked an ad, then bought within seven days) brings it from 203 purchases / €88,776 down to 52 purchases / €18,979. So roughly 79% of what Meta claims this period is view-through, up from 74% in the last report. The remaining 52 is still about 5 times the 11 orders WooCommerce can verify.
The honest rangeThe verified floor is €3,772.01 (11 orders). A generous read, allowing for a handful of genuinely Meta-driven purchases that landed as Direct in the two categories only Meta advertises (Compost, Sand+Stones), puts Meta's realistic contribution this period at roughly €3,772 to €4,600, not €88,776. Cost per verified Meta order was €83.24 (€915.64 ÷ 11), down from €350.45 in Aug 1–21 (€3,154.05 ÷ 9), a real efficiency gain even before judging profitability. Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish ePrivacy/GDPR rules, and WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated the same way, so a small number of genuine Meta orders may sit mistagged as Direct or Organic.

Limitations to keep in mindThe verified floor is a floor, not an exact count. WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated the same way Meta's pixel is, so some genuine Meta-driven purchases may sit mistagged as Direct or Organic. That is exactly what the September 25 test is designed to catch: if firewood, compost or sand revenue drops noticeably now that most of Meta is off, Meta was contributing something the last-click data couldn't see. If it holds, the paused spend was waste. Heating oil and gas are the safe ones (Google dominates); firewood, compost and sand are the real tests. The loss narrowing this sharply on 71% less spend is itself a strong early signal for the latter answer.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" (ex-VAT, ex-shipping). Meta-attributed = orders tagged "Source: [DV]...", "Source: Paid+Social", or a Facebook.com referral, in the WooCommerce Attribution field. 11 such orders Sep 1–21, 9 Aug 1–21.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under GDPR/ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this piece, including the Conversions API, which only improves match quality for visitors who already consented.
  • The verified figure is a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine is also gated behind cookie consent, so some genuinely Meta-driven purchases land as Direct or Organic instead.
  • Meta's reported value: includes view-through conversions (ad seen, not clicked, purchase made later) and cannot be reconciled to real orders on this account. Shown as a ceiling only.
  • Category margins: supplied by you (firewood 25%, heating oil 10%, compost 25%, sand and stones 25%).
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting API for the exact Sep 1–21 / Aug 1–21 windows, Sep 22 2026.
