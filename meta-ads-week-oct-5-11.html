Methodology & caveats

  • Cross-check: the order export was checked against WooCommerce’s own Products report for the same dates (€115,479.00 and 329 items against €115,478.99 and 329 items), and Meta's spend, clicks and claimed purchases for the FlexVolt test were re-pulled on October 12 for both weeks and match the figures above.
  • Real orders basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (excluding VAT and shipping), valid orders only (refunded orders excluded), for Oct 5–11 from a fresh export downloaded October 12. The Sep 28–Oct 4 figures are those published in last week's report.
  • Meta attribution rule: an order counts as Meta-driven if its attribution carries a campaign tag or a Paid+Social tag. This week I did not count plain Facebook referrals, because no ad was running when they came in; earlier reports counted them. Generic Instagram or Facebook source tags without a campaign are never counted, so verified figures are a floor.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you. The one counted order is heating oil (10%).
  • Consent and tracking limit: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot be tracked for ad attribution by any method, so the gap between Meta's claim and your orders is partly that and partly Meta counting views that never led to a click.
  • Live platform data: pulled from Meta for the exact windows on October 12, 2026.
