The headline

ReadReal net loss on verified Meta orders narrowed from −€3,483.16 in August to between −€450.21 and −€96.25 in September, while spend fell 77.5% (€4,722.85 to €1,062.75). The Sep 4 cut to near-zero spend is the reason. Cost of ad spend per verified Meta order fell from €236.14 to €81.75. September's real revenue from Meta-tagged orders was €4,027.51 on 13 orders, against €8,127.59 on 20 orders in August, and the weekly checks I ran through the month show the revenue the account earns elsewhere did not fall when the spend did.

The decision this month: the incrementality read landed and confirms the cut

ReadKeeping Meta near-zero was the right call, and Firewood (business customers) specifically should be retired. Real orders by category, Sep 4–27 against the Aug 5–Sep 3 baseline (the read completed on Sep 28): Firewood +45.9%, Gas +45.0% and Sand+Stones −1.3% per day, all with essentially zero live Meta spend behind them, so nothing was lost by cutting these. Heating oil rose 42.8% per day, though that is mostly Google and the heating season. Compost showed a genuine ~30% decline, but its campaign never fully zeroed out during the test window, so I am treating it as the one open thread, not a clean read. Firewood (business customers), the one campaign kept live to test this, produced the most decisive finding: see the campaign table below.

Ad spend, answered directly

Read€1,062.75 in September, down 77.5% from €4,722.85 in August. Six of the seven main August campaigns (Heating Oil, Compost, Retargeting, Firewood for households, Sand+Stones and Gas Cylinders) were paused on Sep 4 and show only residual same-day spend since. Firewood (business customers) was deliberately kept live through the test window (€534.20 in September, against €405.90 in August), and the new DEWALT FlexVolt Cut Off Saw test added €26.59 from Sep 28. Combined with Google, total spend across both platforms was €7,946.01 in September against €10,540.16 in August.

A figure at or above 100% is impossible by definition: Meta cannot have driven more revenue than the category earned in total. In August only Sand+Stones was over that line (122%), with Compost (91%) close behind and Firewood (59%) and Heating oil (31%) lower. In September Compost (262%) and Firewood (147%) both went over it, even though total Meta spend fell by three-quarters, while Sand+Stones fell back to 11% and Heating oil to 4%. Claimed value did not shrink in proportion to spend (Compost claimed €11,532.65 on €55.28 of spend), the clearest sign that Meta's figure follows its own attribution model, not real ad-driven behaviour. None of the real orders in Heating oil or Sand+Stones are tagged to Meta this period, so those two lower figures are not confirmed as real either.
Where the inflation comes fromMost of it is view-through: purchases Meta credits itself for because someone saw an ad without clicking it. Counting only people who clicked an ad and bought within seven days, September falls from 237 purchases / €100,681 to 61 purchases / €21,212, so about 79% of the claimed value is view-through. August's click-only figure was 113 purchases / €45,832 against 234 / €92,684, a 51% view-through share. Even September's click-only 61 is more than four times the 13 orders WooCommerce can verify, so click-only alone does not close the gap.
The honest rangeThe verified floor is €4,027.51 (13 orders) for September and €8,127.59 (20 orders) for August. A generous read, allowing for genuinely Meta-driven purchases that landed as Direct in the two categories only Meta advertises (Compost and Sand+Stones: 12 such orders, €1,415.84), puts September's realistic contribution at roughly €4,028 to €5,443, not €100,681. Cost per verified Meta order (spend divided by WooCommerce-verified Meta orders) was €42.51 to €81.75 in September (€1,062.75 ÷ 25 to 13), down from €236.14 in August (€4,722.85 ÷ 20). Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish and European data protection rules, and WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated the same way, so a small number of genuine Meta orders may sit mistagged as Direct or Organic. Total website sales for September were €355,761.60 across 892 orders (August: €201,899.23 across 532); the gap between those totals and the Meta-attributed figure includes both non-Meta traffic and consent-declined Meta visitors, which cannot be separated with current tracking.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" (excluding value added tax and shipping). Meta-attributed means orders carrying a Meta campaign source tag, "Source: Paid+Social", or a Facebook.com referral in the WooCommerce attribution field (generic "Source: Ig" and "Source: Fb" tags are excluded as unconfirmed). 13 such orders in September, 20 in August, from 892 and 532 valid orders respectively.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you (heating oil 10%, firewood 25%, compost 25%, sand and stones 25%). One order in a bundle product with no supplied margin is shown as revenue only, in both months.
  • Comparison basis: full calendar months, Sep 1–30 against Aug 1–31, from fresh WooCommerce exports and live Meta pulls for their exact windows.
  • Whole-category revenue: real category revenue comes from the WooCommerce products reports for Sep 1–30 and Aug 1–31 (one row per product, so nothing is double-counted). Sand+Stones is everything filed under Gardening & Landscaping that is not Compost.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under the General Data Protection Regulation and ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this piece, including the Conversions API, which only improves match quality for visitors who already consented. See the reporting methodology.
  • The verified figure is a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution is also gated behind cookie consent, so some genuinely Meta-driven purchases land as Direct or Organic instead.
  • Meta's reported value includes view-through conversions (ad seen, not clicked, purchase made later) and cannot be reconciled to real orders on this account. Shown as a ceiling only.
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh from Meta for the exact Sep 1–30 and Aug 1–31 windows on Oct 2, 2026.
