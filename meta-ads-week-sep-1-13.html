The decision this period

ReadDirection confirmed September 11: keep Meta running, don't wind it down. The September 4 cut (six of seven campaigns paused, only Firewood B2B kept live) stays in place as a clean measurement test, not as a step toward dropping the channel. The goal now is to fix the measurement gap so Meta gets a fair read, not to justify cutting further. That means chasing the tracking fixes already in motion with Graphedia and reading every campaign's real performance off WooCommerce-tagged orders, not Meta's own dashboard, which this period is claiming purchase values that are, for two of the four active categories, larger than the entire category's real revenue from every channel combined (see the table below). The read date stays September 25: real orders by category, all channels, compared against the pre-cut baseline.

A figure at or above 100% is impossible by definition: Meta can't have driven more revenue than the category earned in total. Compost and Firewood both fail that test this period. Heating oil (11%) and Sand+Stones (40%) sit in a range that could plausibly be real, though neither is confirmed: none of the real orders in either category this period are actually tagged to Meta.
Where the inflation comes fromMost of it is view-through: purchases Meta credits itself for because the person saw an ad without clicking it. Re-running this period's figures on a click-only basis (only counting people who actually clicked an ad, then bought within seven days) brings it from 144 purchases / €59,604 down to 41 purchases / €15,511. So roughly 74% of what Meta claims this period is view-through. The remaining 41 is still about seven times the six orders WooCommerce can verify. Reporting continues on the click-only basis, and any campaign rebuilt after the September 25 review will be set to click-only from the start (Meta doesn't allow this setting to be changed on a campaign that's already running).
The honest rangeThe verified floor is €2,651.55 (6 orders). None of the real Compost or Sand+Stones orders this period are Meta-tagged, so there's little basis to add a ceiling for either. A generous read, allowing for a handful of genuinely Meta-driven purchases that landed as Direct or Organic, puts Meta's realistic contribution this period at roughly €2,650 to €4,000, not €59,604. Cost per verified Meta order was €126.07 (€756.39 ÷ 6), against €392.44 in Aug 1–13 (€1,962.19 ÷ 5). Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish ePrivacy/GDPR rules, and WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated the same way, so a small number of genuine Meta orders may sit mistagged as Direct or Organic.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" (ex-VAT, ex-shipping). Meta-attributed = orders tagged "Source: Paid Social", "Source: Ig/Fb", a Meta campaign name, or a Facebook.com referral, in the WooCommerce Attribution field. 6 such orders Sep 1–13, 5 Aug 1–13.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under GDPR/ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this piece, including the Conversions API, which only improves match quality for visitors who already consented.
  • The verified figure is a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine (Sourcebuster JS) is also gated behind cookie consent, so some genuinely Meta-driven purchases land as Direct or Organic instead.
  • Meta's reported value: includes view-through conversions (ad seen, not clicked, purchase made later) and cannot be reconciled to real orders on this account. Shown as a ceiling only.
  • Category margins: supplied by you (firewood 25%, heating oil 10%, compost 25%, sand and stones 25%).
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting API for the exact Sep 1–13 / Aug 1–13 windows, Sep 15 2026. Full method: reporting-methodology.html.
