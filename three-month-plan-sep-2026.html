The one thing to get straight first: which "ROAS"
PLATFORMWhat the Google and Meta dashboards show (about 28x and 19x in August). This is not a real number. Google counts phone calls at a flat 90 to 220 euro each, and until mid-August it counted solar leads at 12,000 euro each. Meta over-reports purchases by roughly ten times because of the cookie-consent tracking gap. A high platform figure sitting next to a real loss is this mismatch, not a contradiction.
REALVerified revenue from your actual WooCommerce orders, divided by what was spent on ads. In August that was 13.8x on Google and 1.9x on Meta.
NETThe only figure that answers whether the account is making money: real verified revenue, at your real product margins, minus the ad spend. Everything in this report is built on this.

What changed in early September

Three budget moves on September 1, then the Meta cut on September 4
Google Shopping launched, small (Sep 1)
Shopping ads for gas, solid fuel, fencing and hardware went live at a combined 45 euro a day. This advertises product ranges that the search campaigns do not cover at all. It starts deliberately small so only the parts that produce real orders get scaled. First week: about 47 euro a day of spend, one real order so far (hardware), still in the learning period.
Home Heating Oil budget restored to 50 euro a day (Sep 1)
Back to its normal in-season level after the summer. Even at 25 euro a day it was losing nearly half its available impressions to budget in August as demand started to turn. This is the largest real-revenue category, and it is already carrying the account result in September.
Google conversion tracking cleaned up (Sep 3)
Several campaigns were bidding on the wrong signal: phone calls valued at a flat 90 euro each, and in one case broader "AI Max" matching pulling in off-category search terms. Both were tightened to bid on real website purchases instead. This is a meaningful part of why Google's real return on ad spend has kept climbing through September (13.8x in August to 20.4x in the first ten days).
Meta spend cut to about 20 euro a day (Sep 4)
This is the Meta decision, brought forward. A 30-day check to September 3 showed Meta spending about 153 euro a day and producing roughly a 3,200 euro a month real loss on what could be verified. I paused six of the seven campaigns and kept only Firewood on the trade side, which is the one line with a real, traceable return every month. That is not a wind-down of Meta: the priority right now is getting Meta's own measurement fixed (see below) so the channel can be judged fairly, not scaled down further while blind. Full detail is in the September 4 note already sent, and in the section below.

The Meta decision, and where it now stands
Done on September 4: Meta cut to about 20 euro a day, one campaign kept
Rather than carry the Meta loss into another month while the measurement question sat with Niall, I cut it. Six of the seven campaigns are paused. The one kept live is Firewood on the trade side, at about 20 euro a day, because it is the only Meta line with a real, traceable return every month. This saves roughly 4,000 euro a month against August. This is a pause on the underperforming lines, not a decision to wind Meta down: the active work right now is fixing what Meta can measure, so the channel gets judged on real numbers rather than cut further while blind.
Now running: a three-week test, read on September 25
With Meta almost off, I am watching whether real sales in the categories it used to advertise (compost, sand and stones, gas, some firewood) hold up or drop. If a category holds, that Meta spend was waste and stays off. If a category drops in a way that lines up with the pause, Meta was contributing there through sales the tracking could not see, and I rebuild just that category with proper measurement in place. Early signal through Sep 10: firewood revenue is holding and growing strongly, compost is a little softer but on tiny volume. Nothing yet that argues for turning Meta back on before the read.
The tracking fix: verification in progress
Niall reported on September 4 that the Conversions API and click-identifier fix is done. Once confirmed, the September 25 read will be much sharper, and any Meta rebuild after that starts with trustworthy numbers rather than a blind spot.

How the numbers are worked out

  • Real revenue: WooCommerce "Net Sales" (excluding VAT and shipping), matched to the channel by the site's own attribution tags, product category, and coupon codes.
  • Real net profit: real verified revenue multiplied by your real product margins (heating oil 10 percent, gas 20 percent, hardware and fencing 15 percent, firewood and compost 25 percent), minus the platform-reported ad spend.
  • Ranges, not single numbers: about a third of orders do not carry product detail in the export, so category margins carry some uncertainty. The channel split (Google versus Meta versus Direct) is solid regardless.
  • Kenergy Solar is excluded from all profit maths: it is a quote-based install service with no online checkout to verify against.
  • Full monthly detail: the August Google Ads and Meta Ads reports carry the campaign-level breakdown behind these totals.
