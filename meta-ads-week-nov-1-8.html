A note on how orders are counted

NoteAn order counts as Meta-driven if it carries a Meta advertising tag or arrived from a Facebook link. That gives no orders for 1–8 November and 4 orders (€1,161.60) for the same days of October, counted the same way in both. None of October’s 4 carried an advertising tag: all four came from plain Facebook links, and three of them (8 October) arrived when no Meta ad was running, so they are most likely unpaid Facebook activity. I therefore show a second, stricter result that counts only tagged orders. The real result sits between the two.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real orders basis: WooCommerce “Net Sales” column (excluding VAT and shipping), completed and processing orders only, for Nov 1–8 (export downloaded November 9) and Oct 1–8 (taken from the October 1 to November 5 export downloaded November 6). November was checked against WooCommerce’s own Products report for November 1–9: the orders file is 0.29% lower (€122,180.31 against €122,540.66), and the report window runs a few hours longer. Heating oil agrees closely once every order with no product listed is treated as heating oil.
  • How the method works: see the reporting methodology page.
  • Meta attribution rule: an order counts as Meta-driven if its attribution carries a campaign tag, a Paid+Social, Instagram or Facebook source tag, or a Facebook referral. This matches the earlier reports. The advertising-tagged reading counts only the first group. Plain Facebook referrals may be unpaid posts, so the higher reading is a ceiling where referrals were not ads, and the lower reading is a floor in the ad-tagged sense.
  • Firewood business-to-business final read: the two-week push figures (23 October to 5 November) come from the 6 November review of the same WooCommerce data; profit uses 25% on the six firewood orders and 10% on the one heating oil order.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you (heating oil 10%, firewood 25%).
  • Consent and tracking limit: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot be tracked for ad attribution by any method, so the gap between Meta’s claim and your orders is partly that and partly Meta counting views that never led to a click. This is a permanent limit, not something still being fixed.
  • Account activity: taken from the Meta account’s own change history and campaign records, read on November 9, 2026.
  • Live platform data: pulled from Meta and Google for both windows on November 9, 2026. Meta’s purchase and value figures use each campaign’s own attribution setting, which includes one-day view credit; the click and view split above uses Meta’s 7-day click and 1-day view windows.
