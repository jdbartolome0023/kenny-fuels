A note on how orders are counted

NoteAn order counts as Meta-driven if it carries a Meta advertising tag or arrived from a Facebook link. That gives 13 orders (€5,008.73) for October and 14 (€4,897.58) for September, counted the same way in both. Only 3 of October’s 13 carry an advertising tag, against 8 of September’s 14. The other 10 in October came from plain Facebook links, and 5 of them arrived between 8 and 16 October, when no Meta ad was running, so they are most likely unpaid Facebook activity. I therefore show a second, stricter result that counts only the tagged orders. The real result sits between the two.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real orders basis: WooCommerce “Net Sales” column (excluding VAT and shipping), completed and processing orders only, for Oct 1–25 and Sep 1–25, both from fresh exports downloaded October 26, and checked against WooCommerce’s own Products report: October agrees within 0.09%, while the September export is 1.97% (€6,143.11) lower than that report. Heating oil agrees closely once every order with no product listed is treated as heating oil (confirmed by WooCommerce’s own reports); the remaining difference sits in other products and could not be matched to specific orders, so the September figures could be slightly understated.
  • Year on year: a comparison with October 2025 is not included in this report.
  • How the method works: see the reporting methodology page.
  • Meta attribution rule: an order counts as Meta-driven if its attribution carries a campaign tag, a Paid+Social, Instagram or Facebook source tag, or a Facebook referral. This matches the earlier reports. The advertising-tagged reading counts only the first group. Plain Facebook referrals may be unpaid posts, so the higher reading is a ceiling where referrals were not ads, and the lower reading is a floor in the ad-tagged sense.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you (heating oil 10%, firewood 25%).
  • Consent and tracking limit: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot be tracked for ad attribution by any method, so the gap between Meta’s claim and your orders is partly that and partly Meta counting views that never led to a click. This is a permanent limit, not something still being fixed.
  • Account activity: taken from the Meta account’s own change history, read on October 26, 2026. The history was visible back to October 21.
  • Live platform data: pulled from Meta and Google for both windows on October 26, 2026. Meta’s purchase and value figures use each campaign’s own attribution setting, which for the three campaigns that ran from 21 October includes one-day view credit.
