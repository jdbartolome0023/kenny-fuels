A note on how orders are counted

NoteAn order counts as Meta-driven if it carries a Meta advertising tag or arrived from a Facebook link. That gives 20 orders (€7,448.00) for October and 15 (€5,018.28) for September, counted the same way in both. 9 of October’s 20 carry an advertising tag, against 9 of September’s 15. The other 11 in October came from plain Facebook links, and 5 of them arrived between 6 and 20 October, when no Meta ad was running, so they are most likely unpaid Facebook activity. I therefore show a second, stricter result that counts only the tagged orders. The real result sits between the two.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real orders basis: WooCommerce “Net Sales” column (excluding VAT and shipping), completed and processing orders only, 1090 for October and 891 for September, from fresh exports downloaded November 2 (September is taken from the September 1 to October 31 export by date; the October orders in that export are identical to the October-only export). Orders were checked against WooCommerce’s own Products report: October agrees within 0.36%, while the September orders are 1.84% (€6,668.02) lower than that report. Heating oil agrees closely once every order with no product listed is treated as heating oil (confirmed by WooCommerce’s own reports); the remaining difference sits in other products and could not be matched to specific orders, so the September figures could be slightly understated.
  • Same period last year: October 2025 from wc-orders-report-export-17935740505918.csv, dates checked in code 2025-10-01 to 2025-10-31, 1077 rows, 1061 valid orders (€314,887.36), 16 refunded rows excluded (the 8 repeated order numbers are refund pairs, none among the valid orders). Against WooCommerce’s own Products report (€322,835.84) the orders file is 2.46% (€7,948.48) lower, the same direction and size as in the September 2026 file; the gap sits in heating oil (707 orders naming Kerosene or blank, €229,932.49, against 694 and €235,575.62 in the Products report), so heating oil is slightly understated in 2025 (2.5%), as it is in October 2026 (2.4%), and the year-on-year growth is not materially affected. 698 of the 1061 orders (66%) have a blank Product(s) field in 2025 and are treated as heating oil on the same method as 2026; the Products report supports that at report level (it counts 694 Kerosene orders for October 2025), but it was not checked order by order. Platform spend for 2025 was pulled from Google Ads and Meta on November 2, 2026.
  • September restated: September here is counted with the same rule as October (campaign tag, paid-social tag or Facebook link) from a fresh export: 15 orders and €5,018.28. The September monthly report counted fewer orders on an earlier, narrower rule, so its real-net figures differ slightly from the September row above.
  • How the method works: see the reporting methodology page.
  • Meta attribution rule: an order counts as Meta-driven if its attribution carries a campaign tag, a Paid+Social, Instagram or Facebook source tag, or a Facebook referral. This matches the earlier reports. The advertising-tagged reading counts only the first group. Plain Facebook referrals may be unpaid posts, so the higher reading is a ceiling where referrals were not ads, and the lower reading is a floor in the ad-tagged sense.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you (heating oil 10%, firewood 25%, solid fuel 25%, gas 20%, hardware 15%).
  • Consent and tracking limit: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot be tracked for ad attribution by any method, so the gap between Meta’s claim and your orders is partly that and partly Meta counting views that never led to a click. This is a permanent limit, not something still being fixed.
  • Account activity: taken from the Meta account’s own change history (read on October 26, 2026, visible back to October 21) and from the campaigns’ live settings read on November 2, 2026.
  • Live platform data: pulled from Meta and Google for both months on November 2, 2026. Meta’s purchase and value figures use each campaign’s own attribution setting, which for the campaigns running from 21 October includes one-day view credit. The click and view split uses Meta’s 7-day-click and 1-day-view windows.
  • Checks run before this report: Meta spend per campaign adds up to the account total for both months; claimed purchases and values were reproduced by summing the campaigns; the method reproduces the published 1–25 October report exactly (13 counted orders, €5,008.73); the orders files were checked for duplicate order numbers (the repeats are refund pairs, none among valid orders).
