The headline

ReadReal profit after ad spend rose to +€12,293.48 for 1–25 October, up 26.4% on +€9,728.31 for the same 25 days of September, on real Google-driven revenue up 35.8% (€122,359.25 to €166,221.53). Gross profit rose 26.9% (€15,413.91 to €19,560.69) while ad spend rose 27.8% (€5,685.60 to €7,267.21), so spend grew slightly faster than profit. Profit grew more slowly than revenue because sales moved further toward heating oil at its 10% margin: it was 86% of Google’s real revenue against 80% in September, while firewood fell 8.3% (€15,309.46 to €14,031.53) at a 25% margin. Each Google-driven order cost €18.26 in ad spend, against €20.02, a fall of 8.8%. The gain holds up against the uncertainty in September’s figure (see the methodology notes): even if every euro of the unexplained difference there were Google-driven firewood, September’s real net would be about +€11,264.08, still below October’s.

What changed, and why

Changes made from October 6 to October 23
October 6: budget moved from campaigns with no purchases to the two held back by budget
Hardware Search went from €25 to €10 a day (the figure you gave for the saw), Composite Decking was paused, Home Heating Oil Search went from €80 to €90 and Shopping – Firewood from €15 to €25. Goal: put the money where real orders were coming from.
October 8: Home Heating Oil Search to €100 a day, Shopping – Hardware to €5 a day, Gas Bottle & Cylinders paused
Home Heating Oil Search was losing more than half of its possible impressions to budget while converting well, so it went from €90 to €100. Over 1–25 October it showed on 34% of possible searches and lost 42% to budget, so it is still budget-limited. Shopping – Hardware was returning about 2.5 times its spend where about 6.7 times is needed to break even at a 15% margin, so it went from €15 to €5. Gas Bottle was paused after it spent €642 since 1 August for 1.9 purchases Google Analytics 4 credited to it.
October 8: phone number removed from the heating oil ads
One headline in each of the three Home Heating Oil ads contained a phone number, which Google does not allow in ad text and which left the ads only partly approved. I removed it, and all three are now fully approved.
October 8, 12 and 19: delivery settings applied to product listings
Heating oil, kiln dried firewood crate and other listings were being held out of Shopping because they arrived without a delivery setting. I applied the setting to the current listings on all three dates, 214 listings on 19 October alone. Shopping – Heating Oil, which spent €11.58 in September, spent €44.90 in October and recorded 2 conversions.
October 23: firewood push for the next two weeks, across all of Ireland
As you asked, I am pushing firewood hard until 6 November. Shopping – Firewood went to a €0.60 maximum bid (from €0.40) and €50 a day (from €25), showing to people in Ireland, and the Firewood Search campaign was switched back on at €20 a day with a €1.00 cap per click. Goal: reach more firewood buyers nationwide for two weeks, then judge it on real Google-tagged firewood orders against the baseline of about €526.07 a day of firewood revenue before the push.
October 23: Hardware Search paused, and Gas Bottle & Cylinders restarted for a one-week test
Hardware Search (€10 a day, which also holds the FlexVolt saw ad group) was paused because it had produced almost no conversions. Gas Bottle was switched back on at €10 a day with exact-match keywords only, six negative keywords and the propane page as its landing page. Google-tagged gas orders ran at 1.14 a day with the campaign on (1 to 7 October) and 0.40 a day with it off (8 to 22 October). I will keep it if the week to 30 October gives about one order a day or more, and pause it again if it gives four or fewer.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (excluding VAT and shipping), 873 valid orders for Oct 1–25 and 749 for Sep 1–25, both from fresh exports downloaded October 26 (refunded orders excluded). Orders were checked against WooCommerce's own Products report for the same dates: October agrees within 0.09% (€360,292.89 against €359,965.47). The September export is 1.97% (€6,143.11) lower than the Products report, the same direction and size as in the Oct 1–18 report; I could not match it to specific orders. If all of it were Google-driven firewood at the 25% margin, September’s real net would be up to about €1,535.78 higher, which would still leave it below October’s. Heating oil agrees closely with WooCommerce’s own kerosene totals once every order with no product listed is treated as heating oil (October: 506 orders and €274,587.40 against 512 and €279,587.40; September: 369 and €206,813.10 against 367 and €209,627.90).
  • Year on year: a comparison with October 2025 is not included in this report.
  • Comparison basis: Oct 1–25 is compared with the same 25 days of September, using live Google Ads data for each window's exact dates. The two periods are not Monday-to-Sunday weeks, so this report does not replace the weekly numbers.
  • Channel attribution: orders tagged exactly "Source: Google" are counted as Google-driven. "Organic: Google" (unpaid search: 209 orders, €83,952.48 in October) is never added to this total.
  • Tagging caveat: paid Google clicks have carried tracking tags since the fix on 8 July, so "Source: Google" now mostly means a paid click. The tag comes from WooCommerce's own order attribution. It can include some visits that were not ad clicks and miss others (visitors who decline cookies), so treat the verified figure as a close estimate of Google-driven sales, not an exact count.
  • Standing exclusion: Compost and Sand and Stones revenue tagged "Source: Google" is excluded (€3,305.75 in October), since no Google Ads campaign targets those categories. Widening that rule would add at most about €826.44 of profit and would not change any budget decision.
  • Category resolution: every order was categorised once, before any date split. A share of orders carry no product details in the export (414 of October’s 873, 345 of September’s 749), almost entirely heating oil, and are resolved to Heating oil on the same verified method as every earlier report (WooCommerce’s own kerosene order filter and variations reports; 278 of 278 blank orders checked in the shop admin were heating oil), so profit on them is at the lowest margin (10%) and cannot be overstated. Named products that fit no category (bulk deals, lubricants, coolant, pumps) are shown as revenue only.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you (heating oil 10%, firewood 25%, solid fuel 25%, gas 20%, hardware 15%).
  • Platform figures still maturing: Google keeps crediting conversions for several days after a period ends, so October's Google-reported figures will most likely rise. Verified (WooCommerce) figures are not affected.
  • Checks run before this report: ad spend was compared between the account total and the sum of the campaigns for both windows and ties to the cent; Google’s reported conversion value was split by conversion type and ties to the account total; the orders files were checked for duplicate order numbers, zero or negative sales and date coverage.
  • Kenergy Solar: excluded from all real-profit math and every real-vs-platform ratio.
  • Live platform data: pulled from Google Ads for both windows on October 26, 2026.
