The headline

ReadReal profit after ad spend rose to +€4,894.62 for 1–8 November, up 16.7% on +€4,193.50 for the same 8 days of October, on real Google-driven revenue up 6.6% (€55,972.01 to €59,676.43) while ad spend fell 7.9% (€2,550.27 to €2,348.00). Gross profit rose 7.4% (€6,743.77 to €7,242.62). Each Google-driven order cost €16.08 in ad spend, against €19.47, a fall of 17.4%. Heating oil was 81% of Google’s real revenue (85% last October), firewood rose 8.7% (€5,503.96 to €5,982.35) at its 25% margin, and gas and coal both grew. Eight days is a short window, so read the direction rather than the exact percentages. One caution: the Google tag in WooCommerce also covers visits from your free Google Business Profile listing, which Google Analytics labels separately from ads. Of the Google-tagged orders Analytics could identify, 23 (€9,436.82) came from the listing, against 8 (€3,013.68) in the October days. Taking those out, real net profit is +€3,956.02 against +€3,852.41 (+2.7%), so the safer reading is that profit held about level while spend fell, rather than that it grew sharply.

ReadGoogle’s own number fell to 1.03x the verified figure, from 2.42x, but the fall is in how Google counts, not in sales. Verified Google-driven revenue rose (€55,972.01 to €59,676.43) while Google’s reported value fell 55% (€135,210.88 to €61,217.19). Most of the drop is in the purchases Google credits to its ads: 21.5 purchases worth €19,198.43 against 59.9 worth €78,370.17 in the October days, while WooCommerce shows 146 verified Google-tagged orders against 131. Phone calls, valued at a flat rate and never appearing as orders, were €42,018.77 against €56,840.71. I tested this order by order against Google Analytics and the purchase tracking has not broken: Analytics recorded 49% of all shop orders in both windows. Three things explain the drop. First, Brand Search and Home Heating Oil Search do not run on Sundays, and this window has two Sundays (1 and 8 November) against one (4 October), so Brand Search clicks fell from 2,340 to 1,294. Second, of the in-scope Google-tagged orders, Analytics credits 44 to ads against 61 in the October days, and 23 to the Google Business Profile listing against 8. Third, 71 of the 146 Google-tagged orders were not recorded by Analytics at all (58 of 131 in the October days), so they cannot be placed either way. Real sales and profit above come from WooCommerce and are not affected by any of this.

What changed, and why

Changes made on November 6, after the two-week firewood push
November 6: Home Heating Oil Search budget from €100 to €120 a day, as a test
From 2 to 5 November it lost between 44% and 58% of possible impressions to budget while converting well. Google-tagged heating oil orders had risen from 11.27 a day (1 to 22 October) to 14.2 a day (1 to 5 November), and an average order earns about €50.90 at your 10% margin, so each extra order covers a lot of extra spend. The season is also rising on its own, so I am treating this as a test: if there is no lift beyond the seasonal rise by 13 November I will put it back to €100. Goal: more conversions at a lower cost per order.
November 6: Firewood Search campaign paused; Shopping – Firewood stays at €50 a day
Over the two-week push (23 October to 5 November) Firewood Search spent about €60 with no conversions, so I paused it. Shopping – Firewood spent €723.11 in the push and Google-tagged firewood revenue rose from about €526.00 a day to about €808.00 a day (+54%). All-channel firewood rose only about 7%, so a good part of that lift would probably have happened anyway, and I am not adding budget beyond €50. Goal: keep the part of the push that is working and stop paying for the part that is not.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (excluding VAT and shipping), 319 valid orders for Nov 1–8 (export downloaded November 9) and 286 for Oct 1–8 (taken from the October 1 to November 5 export downloaded November 6); refunded and cancelled orders are excluded. November was checked against WooCommerce’s own Products report for November 1–9: the orders file is 0.29% lower (€122,180.31 against €122,540.66), and the report window runs a few hours longer. Heating oil agrees closely with WooCommerce’s own kerosene total once every order with no product listed is treated as heating oil (€92,872.28 against €92,649.60).
  • Comparison basis: Nov 1–8 is compared with the same 8 days of October, using live Google Ads data for each window’s exact dates. The two periods are not Monday-to-Sunday weeks. Eight days is a short window, so small changes should not be over-read.
  • Channel attribution: orders tagged exactly "Source: Google" are counted as Google-driven. "Organic: Google" (unpaid search: 72 orders, €26,633.39 in this period) is never added to this total.
  • Tagging caveat: paid Google clicks have carried tracking tags since the fix on 8 July, so "Source: Google" now mostly means a paid click. The tag comes from WooCommerce’s own order attribution. It can include some visits that were not ad clicks and miss others (visitors who decline cookies), so treat the verified figure as a close estimate of Google-driven sales, not an exact count. For this period I checked it order by order against Google Analytics: 23 of the in-scope Google-tagged orders (€9,436.82) are labelled as coming from the Google Business Profile listing rather than ads, against 8 (€3,013.68) in the October days, and 71 were not recorded by Analytics, so the verified figure is best read as a ceiling for ad-driven sales.
  • Standing exclusion: Compost and Sand and Stones revenue tagged "Source: Google" is excluded (€895.10 in this period), since no Google Ads campaign targets those categories. Two cement orders placed together with gravel fall in this excluded group.
  • Category resolution: every order was categorised once, before any date split. A share of orders carry no product details in the export (151 of this period’s 319, 135 of October’s 286), almost entirely heating oil, and are resolved to Heating oil on the same verified method as every earlier report, so profit on them is at the lowest margin (10%) and cannot be overstated. Named products that fit no category (bulk deals, lubricants, coolant, pumps) are shown as revenue only.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you (heating oil 10%, firewood 25%, solid fuel 25%, gas 20%, hardware 15%).
  • Platform figures still maturing: Google keeps crediting conversions for a few days after a period ends, so the latest days of Google-reported figures can still rise slightly. Verified (WooCommerce) figures are not affected.
  • Checks run before this report: ad spend was compared between the account total and the sum of the campaigns for both windows and ties to the cent; Google’s reported conversion value was split by conversion type (phone calls and purchases) and ties to the account total for both windows.
  • Kenergy Solar: excluded from all real-profit math and every real-vs-platform ratio.
  • Live platform data: pulled from Google Ads for both windows on November 9, 2026.
