A note on how orders are counted

NoteIn this report an order counts as Meta-driven if it carries a Meta advertising tag or arrived from a Facebook link. That gives 7 orders (€2,982.75) for October and 10 (€3,735.03) for September, counted the same way in both. Only one of October’s 7 orders carries an advertising tag (a heating oil order on 9 October, €721.59). The other six came from plain Facebook links, and five of them arrived on 8 October or later, after every Meta campaign had been paused. Those are most likely unpaid Facebook activity, so I show a second, stricter result for October below. Either way the sample is small, so read both periods as “small numbers”, not a trend.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real orders basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (excluding VAT and shipping), valid orders only (refunded orders excluded), for Oct 1–18 and Sep 1–18, both from fresh exports downloaded October 19, and checked against WooCommerce’s own Products report: October agrees within 0.13%, while the September export is 2.2% (€5,539.64) lower than that report. Heating oil agrees within 0.6% once every order with no product listed is treated as heating oil (confirmed by WooCommerce’s own reports); the remaining 1.8% sits in other products and could not be matched to specific orders, so the September figures could be slightly understated.
  • Year on year: a comparison with October 2025 is not included in this report.
  • How the method works: see the reporting methodology page.
  • Meta attribution rule: an order counts as Meta-driven if its attribution carries a campaign tag, a Paid+Social tag, or a Facebook referral. This matches the earlier September reports. The Oct 5–11 report did not count plain Facebook referrals, because no ad was running when they came in; both periods here use the same rule so they can be compared. Generic Instagram or Facebook source tags without a campaign are not counted, so verified figures are a floor in the ad-tagged sense, and a ceiling where referrals were not ads.
  • Gross profit: category margins supplied by you (heating oil 10%, firewood 25%).
  • Consent and tracking limit: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot be tracked for ad attribution by any method, so the gap between Meta’s claim and your orders is partly that and partly Meta counting views that never led to a click.
  • Live platform data: pulled from Meta for both windows on October 19, 2026.
