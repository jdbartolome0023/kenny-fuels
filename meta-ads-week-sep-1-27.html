The decision this period: the incrementality read landed, and it confirms the cut

ReadThe September 25 test that this report series has been building toward is now complete: keeping Meta near-zero was the right call, and Firewood B2B specifically should be retired for good. Real orders by category, Sep 4–27 vs the Aug 5–Sep 3 baseline: Firewood, Gas and Sand+Stones all held or grew with essentially zero live Meta spend behind them (+45.9%, +45.0%, -1.3%). Nothing was lost by cutting these. Compost showed a genuine ~30% real decline, but its own campaign never fully zeroed out during the test window, so it isn't a clean read, flagged as the one open thread, not acted on. Firewood B2B, the one campaign kept live specifically to test this, produced the most decisive finding of the whole three-week exercise: see the campaign table below.

A figure at or above 100% is impossible by definition: Meta can't have driven more revenue than the category earned in total. None of the four categories had crossed that line in August: Compost (91%), Sand & stones (92%) and Firewood (57%) were all inflated but still below it, and Heating oil (31%) sat lower still. By September, Compost jumped to 267% and Firewood to 164%, both now decisively over the line, even as Firewood's own spend barely changed and total Meta spend fell by three-quarters. The inflation got worse as spend fell, the clearest sign yet that Meta's claimed value tracks its own attribution model, not real ad-driven behaviour. Heating oil (4%) and Sand+Stones (12%) in September sit in a range that could plausibly be real, though neither is confirmed: none of the real orders in either category this period are actually tagged to Meta.
Where the inflation comes fromMost of it is view-through: purchases Meta credits itself for because the person saw an ad without clicking it. Re-running September's figures on a click-only basis (only counting people who actually clicked an ad, then bought within seven days) brings it from 231 purchases / €99,986 down to 56 purchases / €19,549, meaning roughly 80% of what Meta claims is view-through. August's click-only figure was 90 purchases / €37,722 against its 196-purchase/€78,301 total claim, a smaller but still substantial 54% view-through share. Even September's click-only 56 is about 4 to 5 times the 13 orders WooCommerce can verify, so click-only alone doesn't close the gap.
The honest rangeThe verified floor is €4,027.51 (13 orders) for September, €6,973.32 (16 orders) for August. A generous read, allowing for a handful of genuinely Meta-driven purchases that landed as Direct in the two categories only Meta advertises (Compost, Sand+Stones), puts Meta's realistic contribution in September at roughly €4,028 to €5,247, not €99,986. Cost per verified Meta order was €79.68 in September (€1,035.84 ÷ 13), down sharply from €258.16 in August (€4,130.60 ÷ 16). Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish ePrivacy/GDPR rules, and WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated the same way, so a small number of genuine Meta orders may sit mistagged as Direct or Organic.

Limitations to keep in mindThe verified floor is a floor, not an exact count, and August's −€3,136.08 is presented as a single verified figure rather than a floor-to-ceiling range, unlike September's. August ran seven campaigns at full scale, spanning categories (Heating Oil, BOF Retargeting, Gas Cylinders) where the same generous allowance used for September's range doesn't apply cleanly, so a single verified floor is the more honest number rather than an extrapolated ceiling. WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated the same way Meta's pixel is, so some genuine Meta-driven purchases may sit mistagged as Direct or Organic in both months. The Sep 25 incrementality read (see above) is the real answer to whether that matters going forward: Firewood, Gas and Sand+Stones all held or grew once August's full spend was cut, meaning the mistagging risk did not translate into lost revenue. The comparison between the two months is the clearest evidence yet that the cut was the right call: real net loss narrowed from −€3,136.08 to a range of −€423 to −€118 while spend fell 74.9%.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" (ex-VAT, ex-shipping). Meta-attributed = orders tagged "Source: [DV]...", "Source: Paid+Social", or a Facebook.com referral, in the WooCommerce Attribution field ("Source: Ig"/"Source: Fb" generic social tags excluded as unconfirmed). 13 such orders Sep 1–27, 16 Aug 1–27.
  • Comparison basis: Aug 1–27 vs Sep 1–27, a genuine 27-day-to-27-day month-over-month comparison, both built from fresh WooCommerce exports and live Meta API pulls for their own exact windows. August's real net is shown as a single verified floor rather than a floor/ceiling range (see the net-profit section above for why).
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under GDPR/ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this piece, including the Conversions API, which only improves match quality for visitors who already consented.
  • The verified figure is a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine is also gated behind cookie consent, so some genuinely Meta-driven purchases land as Direct or Organic instead.
  • Meta's reported value: includes view-through conversions (ad seen, not clicked, purchase made later) and cannot be reconciled to real orders on this account. Shown as a ceiling only.
  • Category margins: supplied by you (firewood 25%, heating oil 10%, compost 25%, sand and stones 25%).
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting API for the exact Sep 1–27 and Aug 1–27 windows, Sep 29 2026.
