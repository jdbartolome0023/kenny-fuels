A figure at or above 100% is impossible by definition: Meta can't have driven more revenue than the category earned in total. Compost and Firewood both fail that test on the full 27-day window, and Firewood specifically has gone from 173% at the Sep 1–21 checkpoint to 164% now, still decisively over the line even as more real firewood revenue has come in. Heating oil (4%) and Sand+Stones (12%) sit in a range that could plausibly be real, though neither is confirmed: none of the real orders in either category this period are actually tagged to Meta.
Where the inflation comes fromMost of it is view-through: purchases Meta credits itself for because the person saw an ad without clicking it. Re-running this period's figures on a click-only basis (only counting people who actually clicked an ad, then bought within seven days) brings it from 231 purchases / €99,986 down to 56 purchases / €19,549. So roughly 80% of what Meta claims this period is view-through, up slightly from 79% at the Sep 1–21 checkpoint. The remaining 56 is still about 4 to 5 times the 13 orders WooCommerce can verify.
The honest rangeThe verified floor is €4,027.51 (13 orders). A generous read, allowing for a handful of genuinely Meta-driven purchases that landed as Direct in the two categories only Meta advertises (Compost, Sand+Stones), puts Meta's realistic contribution this period at roughly €4,028 to €5,247, not €99,986. Cost per verified Meta order was €79.68 (€1,035.84 ÷ 13), down slightly from €83.24 at the Sep 1–21 checkpoint. Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish ePrivacy/GDPR rules, and WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated the same way, so a small number of genuine Meta orders may sit mistagged as Direct or Organic.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" (ex-VAT, ex-shipping). Meta-attributed = orders tagged "Source: [DV]...", "Source: Paid+Social", or a Facebook.com referral, in the WooCommerce Attribution field. 13 such orders Sep 1–27, 11 at the Sep 1–21 checkpoint.
  • Comparison basis: this report compares against the account's own Sep 1–21 checkpoint (published Sep 22), not an equivalent Aug 1–27 window, since no Aug 1–27 order-level export was available at build time.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under GDPR/ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this piece, including the Conversions API, which only improves match quality for visitors who already consented.
  • The verified figure is a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine is also gated behind cookie consent, so some genuinely Meta-driven purchases land as Direct or Organic instead.
  • Meta's reported value: includes view-through conversions (ad seen, not clicked, purchase made later) and cannot be reconciled to real orders on this account. Shown as a ceiling only.
  • Category margins: supplied by you (firewood 25%, heating oil 10%, compost 25%, sand and stones 25%).
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting API for the exact Sep 1–27 / Sep 1–21 windows, Sep 29 2026.
